# Chapter 1: Fields, Vector Spaces, and Subspaces

## 1. What problem does this chapter aim to solve?

**Core mission:** Understand what kinds of objects linear algebra can act on.

## 2. Chapter Roadmap

### First Leap (Fields $\mathbb{F}$)

- This chapter defines **$\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{C}$** along with the operations on them, proves the **laws of arithmetic**, and shows that the **complex numbers** and the **real numbers** are **closed under these operations**. It then represents **vectors as a sequence together with its length** and defines operations on vectors.

### Second Leap (Vector Spaces $V$)

- This chapter defines **vector spaces** with **closed addition and scalar multiplication**, and also presents the **laws of arithmetic**. An interesting point is that **$F^S$** is the set of functions from $S$ to $F$, and **vectors can also be viewed from this function perspective**. Then it proves some **properties of vector spaces**.

### Third Leap (Subspaces)

- When dealing with a vector space $V$, we sometimes consider a subset of $V$ that is itself a vector space. This chapter defines **subspaces** as **nonempty subsets closed under addition and scalar multiplication**, and then introduces the **sum** and **direct sum** of subspaces.

## 3. Summary

> **Vector spaces do not care about the nature of their elements; they only focus on the algebraic operations and their properties. Subspaces are subsets of a vector space that are themselves vector spaces under the same operations.**
